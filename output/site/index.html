The ground game

Where the seats actually live

The forecast poststratified through the polling-station record: official per-locality results for 2019–2022 cluster Israel's localities into political sectors, and each 2026 list's projected seats are allocated to the sectors it draws from via measured voter-flow lineages. Turnout per sector is measured from the same administrative data — this is what makes a national number physically checkable: a list cannot take more of a sector's votes than that sector casts.

The polling industry

Two polling worlds, one model

Israeli pollsters disagree by up to ten seats on the bloc totals. The model estimates each firm's lean (its house effect on the Netanyahu bloc, in seats, measured leave-own-pollster-out) and each firm's historical accuracy, and weights accordingly. Firms with shared ownership or methodology are modeled as correlated voices, not independent ones.

Methodology

The statistical machinery, end to end

The pipeline below is a genuine sequence — each stage consumes the last. All code-derived parameters are measured on nine election cycles (2009–2022) with leave-one-cycle-out discipline; all judgment parameters are stated, cited, and stress-tested.

The poll database

1,857 polls, 22,681 list-level observations, nine cycles (2009–2026), scraped from the Wikipedia polling archives with a purpose-built parser (grid/colspan handling, two-digit years, US-style dates, result-row detection), then reconciled against a canonical party registry that tracks every list's mergers, splits, and bloc identity across 17 years — so "Yamina 2021" and "New Right 2019" resolve to comparable objects. For the 2026 cycle, the raw poll PDFs that pollsters must legally file with the Central Elections Committee are scraped directly from gov.il (19 filings so far); where a CEC filing matches a published poll, the legal filing's raw vote shares displace the seat-derived numbers.

House effects and firm reliability

Each firm's bloc lean is estimated leave-own-pollster-out against the contemporaneous average of all other firms, with empirical-Bayes shrinkage for small samples. Firms with common ownership or lineage — the two halves of the former Filber/Direct Polls operation, Lazar/Panels Politics — share a correlation group: their errors are modeled as one voice, so five agreeing polls from one polling family never masquerade as five independent confirmations. Accuracy scorecards from eight graded cycles set each firm's precision prior.

The Bayesian state-space model

The production model is a latent-trajectory model fitted with NUTS (PyMC). Each list's true support follows a random walk in log-ratio space on a non-uniform time grid — weekly bins, tightening to 2-day bins in the final month — and the projection's forward uncertainty follows a measured volatility calendar: the list-formation window (D-36–77) moves 1.3–1.5 seats/week in the historical record, the final five weeks only 0.6–0.75, so intervals tighten automatically once lists close on September 9. (Poll herding was measured — final-week dispersion runs ~0.88× mid-campaign — and deliberately not double-counted: the election-day shocks are fitted on final-poll errors that already contain it.)

zt = zt−1 + √Δt · τ · εt    latent support, ALR space
yp,ℓ ~ StudentT(4, softmax(zt(p))ℓ + βg(p) + δf(p), σp)    poll observation
σp = sobs / √λf(p) · exp(βu · up)    noise scales with firm record + undecideds

House effects β nest within pollster correlation groups; firm weights λ carry LogNormal priors centered on each firm's historical accuracy; βu is an estimated coefficient letting reported undecided shares widen a poll's noise (posterior: ×1.01 noise per +5pp undecided). The current fit: 0 divergences, r̂ ≤ 1.02, and a live holdout — refit without the last 14 days — predicting the held-out polls with 0.72 seats median error.

The error decomposition

Polls miss elections in structured ways. A sequential decomposition of the eight graded cycles (each estimated leave-one-cycle-out, so no cycle grades itself) splits final-poll error into orthogonal layers, which become the projection's shocks — nothing is double-counted:

  • Bloc swing — sd 3.36 seats, the correlated right/left error (t-distributed, df 5), scaled ×1.2 this cycle for elevated event risk;
  • Sector shocks — haredi lists underpolled by 1.35 ± 1.77 seats; Arab lists −0.32 ± 1.45 (a turnout error, not a bias);
  • Anchors — Likud beats its polls by 2.30 seats on average (the "gevalt" record: +8.4 in 2015, +6.7 in April 2019, ≈0 elsewhere); the poll-leading party underperforms by 1.74;
  • Residual per-list shock — 1.83 + 0.044·seats, with a ×1.35 multiplier for first-cycle lists (measured: new parties' final polls are unbiased but 35% noisier) and a loyal-electorate calibration for sect-based veteran lists — Shas has never missed its final polls downward by more than half a seat in eight cycles, so Shas, UTJ and Yisrael Beytenu are shrunk to their measured historical volatility (×1.5 novelty buffer) instead of the pooled width;
  • Measured undecided break — the 2026 undecided pool (12% of the electorate) leans 55/16 toward a non-Netanyahu candidate (N12/Midgam), a sign reversal of the historical late-break; applied at half weight (~1.6 seats, sd equal to the mean) toward the opposition bloc, with the fragility disclosed: the lean rests on ~60 respondents and is remeasured as new certainty polling arrives;
  • Gevalt coupling — a zero-sum transfer (sd 2.0 seats) between Likud and its own bloc's satellites, so satellite threshold deaths co-occur with Likud surges, as they did in 2015 and 2019.

The kalpi substrate

Beneath the national numbers sits the polling-station record: official CEC results for every locality in the five elections 2019–2022, matched to party lineages through each cycle's ballot letters (verified against national totals, so a wrong letter cannot silently poison the panel). Localities cluster into political sectors by their own vote profiles — Israeli electoral geography is segregated enough that the profiles recover the sectors almost exactly, along with their measured turnout (haredi 76%, Arab 54%, religious-Zionist 76%). Three products feed the forecast: a poststratification table allocating each 2026 list's projected seats to the sectors it draws from — and enforced capacity truncation of the posterior: simulations where the Arab lists jointly exceed what Arab-sector turnout can physically cast (~16.3% of the vote), or where Shas+UTJ fall below the haredi demographic floor (~6.7%), are clipped to the feasible boundary (each pruned ~1.5% of draws); a measured +0.92 correlation between the haredi and Arab sector tides (both turnout-machine sectors), shrunk to 0.5 for four observed transitions and wired into the projection's family shocks, which were previously drawn independently; and the locality baseline for the election-night engine. One honest negative result is recorded alongside: sector-level swings do not transfer across election pairs (leave-one-out: uniform national swing predicts localities at 1.20pp mean error, adding historical sector swings gives 1.31pp) — each cycle's sector story is its own, so the sector layer informs correlations and capacity, not point swings. The lineage weights are additionally calibrated against the measured kalpi-level voter-transition matrices of the open-source Kolot Nodedim project (constrained convex estimation over ~7,000 matched stations per election pair, R² 0.89 for 2021→2022), whose per-party Dirichlet-α cohesion estimates independently reproduce this model's loyal-electorate ordering — and whose overdispersion measurements (a 340-voter kalpi behaves like only 30–120 independent draws) will power the election-night engine's noise model in October.

Threshold and Bader–Ofer

Each simulated vote share vector passes the 3.25% threshold test, then seats are allocated by the actual Israeli method: Bader–Ofer (d'Hondt over remainders), with signed surplus-vote agreements treated as joint lists at the remainder stage — worth a measured +0.5 to +0.9 expected seats per pair. Threshold failures redistribute their votes to survivors, which is how a bloc can lose the vote and win the Knesset.

The registration mixture

Until lists close on September 9, the forecast samples registration events per simulation: the Smotrich–Feiglin technical bloc (0.31), the RZP-into-Likud absorption (0.36), the centre micro-list folds and the Erdan–Gantz union tail (0.12), each with sourced transfer vectors. Resolved events collapse out of the mixture as they happen — Segalovitz joining Ra'am became official on September 1 and is now a certainty in every draw. The published forecast is the mixture; each event's marginal effect is read off the same draws (see the scenario board above). On filing day the mixture collapses to the real configuration and the model refits.

The formation tree

Seats are not power. Every simulated Knesset runs through an explicit formation sequence — Netanyahu bloc first, then centre majority, centre+Ra'am, haredi defection, minority-with- abstentions, pledge-break, unity, repeat election — with behavioral parameters derived from institutional research: who actually signs for each party (Shas's Council, UTJ's two rebbes' councils, Ra'am's post-Shura independence), each leader's revealed record at past forks (Liberman's flips, Bennett's 2021 pledge-break, Gantz's 2020), and historical base rates (repeat election followed 2 of 4 deadlocked formations; unity resolved half of them; no minority government has ever been sworn in from day one). Winter's Amcha is coded as an anti-Eisenkot hard edge, not a generic right-bloc member: it can join a Bennett-led centre government but essentially never an Eisenkot-led one.

The PM allocation

Within each winning coalition, the premiership is allocated with rotation-aware probabilities (Bennett was PM with 7 seats in 2021 — largest-party logic is not Israeli logic), flipping when Together outpolls Yashar in a draw. This layer was graded against the eight real formation outcomes since 2009: average log-loss −0.92, and it assigns the realized outcome the highest probability in five of eight cycles.

Expert priors, documented

Every judgment number traces to a written dossier compiled from six research desks: coalition-formation base rates 1992–2022, the complete Israeli polling-error record 1996–2022, party institutions and leader biographies, electoral sociology and vote flows, prediction markets and fundamentals, and final-stretch campaign dynamics. Where the desks disagreed with the model, the model moved; where the market disagrees with the model (Polymarket prices Eisenkot-as-PM at ~50%; we say 33%), the dossier records why we hold: the market's own hung-parliament and government-by-December markets are inconsistent with its headline number.

Validation: the model grades itself

Every layer is gated on an eight-cycle backtest (2013–2022, leave-one-cycle-out; 2009 seeds the priors). The harness re-runs the full pipeline as of each election eve and scores it against the real result. Changes that failed the harness were rejected and documented — including a final-stretch dynamics module and a hierarchical multi-cycle fit that stays research-only.
