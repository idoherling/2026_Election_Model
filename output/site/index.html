The ground game

Where the seats actually live

The forecast poststratified through the polling-station record: official per-locality results for 2019–2022 cluster Israel's localities into political sectors, and each 2026 list's projected seats are allocated to the sectors it draws from via measured voter-flow lineages. Turnout per sector is measured from the same administrative data — this is what makes a national number physically checkable: a list cannot take more of a sector's votes than that sector casts.

The polling industry

Two polling worlds, one model

Israeli pollsters disagree by up to ten seats on the bloc totals. The model estimates each firm's lean (its house effect on the Netanyahu bloc, in seats, measured leave-own-pollster-out) and each firm's historical accuracy, and weights accordingly. Firms with shared ownership or methodology are modeled as correlated voices, not independent ones.

Methodology

The statistical machinery, end to end

The pipeline below is a genuine sequence — each stage consumes the last. All code-derived parameters are measured on nine election cycles (2009–2022) with leave-one-cycle-out discipline; all judgment parameters are stated, cited, and stress-tested.

The system at a glance

The forecast is one pipeline: four data assets feed a Bayesian poll model; its posterior is pushed through a measured stack of election-day shocks; the resulting vote-share draws are made physically lawful, mixed over the registration events still open, passed through the real seat-allocation law, and finally through an explicit government-formation tree. Every parameter is either measured on the 2009–2022 record (always leave-one-cycle-out) or declared as a documented judgment in the priors dossier. Nothing in the chain is hand-tuned to make the answer look reasonable.

The poll database

1,858 polls, 22,711 list-level observations, nine cycles (2009–2026), scraped from the Wikipedia polling archives with a purpose-built parser (grid/colspan handling, two-digit years, US-style dates, result-row detection), then reconciled against a canonical party registry that tracks every list's mergers, splits, and bloc identity across 17 years — so "Yamina 2021" and "New Right 2019" resolve to comparable objects. For the 2026 cycle, the raw poll reports that pollsters must legally file with the Central Elections Committee are scraped directly from gov.il: 35 filings parsed, and where a filing matches a published poll its raw vote shares displace the seat-derived numbers (23 polls, 299 observations, currently displaced). The small parties that pollsters report only in footnotes are itemized and carried explicitly — see the small-parties chapter. A second observation stream carries sector-specialist polls (Afkar, StatNet): a share of the Arab sector converts to a national share through the sector's measured weight in the vote and the fraction of each list's vote that comes from that sector (kalpi poststrat), and enters the fit as a poll by the sector firm with extra noise for the conversion — precisely the voters mainstream panels under-reach.

vℓ = sharesector · Wsector / concℓ    sector poll → national share (W = eligible × turnout / total votes)

House effects and firm reliability

Each firm's bloc lean is estimated leave-own-pollster-out: firm f's polls are compared with the contemporaneous average of all other firms, then shrunk toward zero in proportion to sample size (empirical Bayes). The chart in the polling-industry section above plots the result. Firms with common ownership or lineage — the two halves of the former Filber/Direct Polls operation, Lazar/Panels Politics — share a correlation group: their errors are modeled as one voice, so five agreeing polls from one polling family never masquerade as five independent confirmations.

δ̂f = nf/(nf+k) · meant( yf,t − ȳ−f,t )    house effect, EB-shrunk
λf ~ LogNormal(accuracyf, σƒ),  σp = sobs·λf·eβuup    precision weight from the final-poll record

Accuracy scorecards from eight graded cycles set each firm's precision prior λ — and the scorecard grades each firm's final pre-election poll against the result (election-day exit polls are excluded, and a firm graded on few cycles is shrunk toward the industry median), so the weight rewards being right when it counts. A firm with a loud record gets a wide noise term; its polls still move the model, just less. One caution the audit surfaced: weekly panel series move only 0.4–0.7 seats a week on the bloc total, far less than their samples' 2–3 seats of pure sampling error — smooth, not accurate — so the precision prior is held tight enough that within-cycle smoothness cannot overturn a firm's graded record. A hard sampling-error floor on every poll was built, tested on the eight-cycle harness, and rejected: it widened already-wide intervals and cost 0.05 seats of accuracy. House effects are not frozen either: each firm's lean carries a drift term — a per-firm slope over the campaign window, centred across firms so a common drift stays in the latent trend — because a firm's methodology and sample can move between April and October.

δf,ℓ(t) = δf,ℓ + sf,ℓ · (t − t̄)/T,  Σf sf,ℓ = 0    time-varying house effect

The Bayesian state-space model

The production model is a latent-trajectory model fitted with NUTS (PyMC). Each list's true support follows a random walk in additive-log-ratio space on a non-uniform time grid — weekly bins, tightening to 2-day bins in the final month. The current 2026 fit consumes 173 polls, 2,188 observations, 15 modeled lists, 45 time bins, 10 firms in 6 correlation groups.

zt = zt−1 + √Δt · τ · εt    latent support, ALR space
yp,ℓ ~ StudentT(4, softmax(zt(p))ℓ + βg(p) + δf(p), σp)    poll observation
σp = sobs / √λf(p) · exp(βu · up)    noise scales with firm record + undecideds

House effects β nest within pollster correlation groups; firm weights λ carry LogNormal priors centered on each firm's historical accuracy; βu is an estimated coefficient letting reported undecided shares widen a poll's noise (posterior: ×1.01 noise per +5pp undecided). The current fit: 0 divergences, r̂ ≤ 1.02, bulk ESS ≥ 332, and a live holdout — refit without the last 14 days — predicting the held-out polls with 0.73 seats median error.

The campaign volatility calendar

Forward uncertainty is not a constant: it follows the calendar. Measured across nine cycles, the list-formation window (77–36 days out) moves 1.3–1.5 seats per list per week; the final five weeks move only 0.6–0.75. The projection's forward variance is scaled accordingly — intervals tighten automatically once lists close on September 9, not because we assume calm but because the historical record says the race calms.

Poll herding was measured and deliberately not double-counted: final-week dispersion runs ~0.88× mid-campaign, but the election-day shocks below are fitted on final-poll errors that already contain it.

  • Sector shocks — haredi lists underpolled by 1.35 ± 1.77 seats; Arab lists −0.32 ± 1.45 (a turnout error, not a bias); the haredi and Arab tides are drawn with correlation 0.5 (measured +0.92 on four transitions, shrunk);

  • Residual per-list shock — 1.83 + 0.044·seats, ×1.35 for first-cycle lists (measured: new parties' final polls are unbiased but 35% noisier), with a loyal-electorate calibration shrinking Shas (1.5), UTJ (1.3), Yisrael Beytenu (2.2), Likud (4.8), the Joint List (3.1) and Yashar (4.2) to their measured total volatility;
  • Certainty layer — each list's soft support (its supporters who are not certain: Together 47% locked and Amcha 38% are measured, the rest declared tiers) churns at a defection rate drawn per simulation (mean 0.25) with list-specific spread, so soft lists carry asymmetric downside (the change bloc's within-bloc wavering measures three times the Netanyahu bloc's); churn lands in proportion to certainty-weighted support, mostly within bloc — historically bloc error is smaller than the sum of its parts — with a tenth crossing and a seventh abstaining (declared). The 12% undecided pool (CEC filings) breaks by the N12 55/16 lean, carried with its own sampling uncertainty (n≈60) and at half weight (~1.6 seats toward the change bloc in expectation);
  • Gevalt coupling — a zero-sum transfer (sd 2.0) between Likud and its own bloc's satellites, so satellite threshold deaths co-occur with Likud surges, as in 2015 and 2019.

The kalpi substrate

Beneath the national numbers sits the polling-station record: official CEC results for every locality in the five elections 2019–2022, matched to party lineages through each cycle's ballot letters (verified against national totals, so a wrong letter cannot silently poison the panel). Localities cluster into political sectors by their own vote profiles, along with their measured turnout (haredi 76%, Arab 54%, religious-Zionist 76%). Three products feed the forecast: a poststratification table allocating each list's seats to the sectors it draws from (the Ground Game section); the capacity truncation below; and the locality baseline for the election-night engine. The lineage weights are calibrated against the measured voter-transition matrices of the open-source Kolot Nodedim project (~7,000 matched stations per election pair, R² 0.89 for 2021→2022), whose per-party cohesion estimates independently reproduce this model's loyal-electorate ordering.

Σℓ∈Arab vℓ ≤ 16.3%    Arab-sector turnout capacity
vShas + vUTJ ≥ 6.7%    haredi demographic floor

Simulations that violate either bound are clipped to the feasible boundary (each condition prunes ~1.5% of draws). One honest negative result is recorded alongside: sector-level swings do not transfer across election pairs (uniform national swing predicts localities at 1.20pp mean error; adding historical sector swings gives 1.31pp) — so the sector layer informs correlations and capacity, not point swings.

The small parties

Lists polled below the threshold are not dropped: each is carried as a micro-list and carved out of the modeled pie before seats are allocated, so the named lists' shares are never silently inflated. Their polled support comes from the itemized footnotes of the pollsters that publish full percentages — but the model does not simply reproduce those numbers. It forecasts each list's actual vote using the measured record of how sub-threshold lists' final polls translated into real ballots: NEP 2021 polled ~1.2% and took 0.79% (×0.66); Jewish Home 2022 polled 1.9% and took 1.17% (×0.62); Yachad 2015 ~3.3% → 2.97% (×0.90); Zehut April 2019 ~3.0% → 2.74% (×0.91); Otzma September 2019 ~1.7% → 1.88% (×1.11). Lists without a committed base shed roughly 30% of their polled support to strategic desertion — voters flee a wasted vote — while lists with a sectarian or ideological core hold theirs. So NEP, Israel First and A Place for All of Us are forecast at 0.7× their polling, The Haredi Public and Noam at 1.0×, and the calibration's own dispersion widens every micro-list's uncertainty. Zehut is no longer carried separately at all: since September 2 the pollsters publish a single Religious Zionism–Zehut column, so its support lives directly inside that line's poll stream.

mj ~ N+(μj, σj),  v′ = ( v·(1−Σjmj), m )    micro-lists carved from the simplex

Threshold and Bader–Ofer

Each simulated vote vector passes the 3.25% threshold test, then seats are allocated by the actual Israeli method — Bader–Ofer, d'Hondt over remainders — with signed surplus-vote agreements treated as joint lists at the remainder stage (worth a measured +0.5 to +0.9 expected seats per pair). Threshold failures redistribute their votes to survivors, which is how a bloc can lose the vote and win the Knesset. The published central seats additionally respect a 4-seat floor for passing lists, reconciled without shaving any list below its own floor.

seatk → argmaxℓ vℓ / (sℓ + 1)    d’Hondt iteration, surplus pairs pooled

The registration mixture

Until lists close on September 9, the forecast samples registration events per simulation: the centre micro-list folds and the Erdan–Gantz union tail, each with sourced transfer vectors. Three events have already resolved and are certainties in every draw: Segalovitz joining Ra'am (September 1), the Smotrich–Feiglin technical bloc (September 2), and Balad remaining inside the Joint List. Still open: a centre union filing (0.12), and the fold decisions of Zionist Home–Reservists (0.45), Blue and White (0.58) and Unity (0.75). The published forecast is the mixture; each event's marginal effect is read off the same draws. On filing day the mixture collapses to the real configuration and the model refits.

P(seats) = Σe P(e) · P(seats | e)    mixture over registration events e

The formation tree

Seats are not power. Every simulated Knesset runs through an explicit formation sequence — an ordered ladder of gates, each with behavioral parameters derived from institutional research: who actually signs for each party (Shas's Council, UTJ's two rebbes' councils, Ra'am's post-Shura independence), each leader's revealed record at past forks (Liberman's flips, Bennett's 2021 pledge-break, Gantz's 2020), and historical base rates. Winter's Amcha is coded as an anti-Eisenkot hard edge: it can join a Bennett-led centre government but essentially never an Eisenkot-led one. The behavioral parameters themselves carry uncertainty — each simulation draws its own set from Beta/Dirichlet priors — so the formation probabilities come with honest intervals, shown in the scenario board. And the gates are configuration-conditional: the declared probabilities are marginal means, and each simulated parliament's gate probability moves with its seat map through a logistic — haredi defection rises with the right's gap to 61 and when that party alone closes the centre's gap (Shas 1992, Shas+UTJ 1999), a centrist defection falls as the gap grows (Gantz 2020 at a gap of three), unity rises with deadlock depth. Graded on the eight real outcomes since 2009 the conditional tree scores the same as the unconditional one (log score −0.93 vs −0.92) — eight outcomes cannot resolve effects of this size — so it is retained on institutional grounds with the marginals preserved by construction.

The PM allocation

Within each winning coalition, the premiership is allocated with rotation-aware, scenario-specific probabilities (Bennett was PM with 7 seats in 2021 — largest-party logic is not Israeli logic), flipping when Together outpolls Yashar in a draw. This layer was graded against the eight real formation outcomes since 2009: average log-loss −0.92, and it assigns the realized outcome the highest probability in five of eight cycles.

PM | outcome ~ Categorical( allocoutcome ),  alloc ~ Dirichlet(60·p̄)    scenario-specific, uncertainty carried

Expert priors, documented

Every judgment number traces to a written dossier compiled from six research desks: coalition-formation base rates 1992–2022, the complete Israeli polling-error record 1996–2022, party institutions and leader biographies, electoral sociology and vote flows, prediction markets and fundamentals, and final-stretch campaign dynamics. Where the desks disagreed with the model, the model moved; where the market disagrees with the model (Polymarket prices Eisenkot-as-PM at ~50%; we say ~36%), the dossier records why we hold: the market's own hung-parliament and government-by-December markets are inconsistent with its headline number.

Validation: the model grades itself

Every layer is gated on an eight-cycle backtest (2013–2022, leave-one-cycle-out; 2009 seeds the priors). The harness re-runs the full pipeline as of each election eve and scores it against the real result. Changes that failed the harness were rejected and documented — including a final-stretch dynamics module and a hierarchical multi-cycle fit that stays research-only.

The two models divide labor: the simple weighted pipeline sets central seats (better point accuracy); the Bayesian model owns every interval and probability (better calibration, and it wins the live 2026 holdout 0.73 vs 1.09 median seats). Blending was tested and rejected — their errors correlate at 0.93, so a blend buys nothing.
