The ground game

Where the seats actually live

The forecast poststratified through the polling-station record: official per-locality results for 2019–2022 cluster Israel's localities into political sectors, and each 2026 list's expected seats — its mean over the simulations, so a list below the threshold today still appears with its fractional expectation — are allocated to the sectors it draws from via measured voter-flow lineages (decision log §9). Turnout per sector is measured from the same administrative data — this is what makes a national number physically checkable: a list cannot take more of a sector's votes than that sector casts.

Track record

How good is this model?

Every election since 2009 was forecast again with the model exactly as it ships, using only the polls published before each date and layer parameters estimated from the other seven elections, some of them later than the one graded , and scored against the real result. A good probabilistic forecast is first calibrated (things it calls 70% likely happen about 70% of the time) and then sharp (its intervals are as narrow as calibration allows) . The charts below show both, from the same backtest that gates every change to the model. Eight elections can check the machinery list by list; they cannot certify a single bloc probability .

The polling industry

Two polling worlds, one model

Israeli pollsters disagree by about 17 seats on the bloc totals: this cycle's house effects on the Netanyahu bloc run from Lazar −5.7 to Filber +11.5 (chart below). The model estimates each firm's lean (its house effect on the Netanyahu bloc, in seats, measured leave-own-pollster-out) and each firm's historical accuracy, and weights accordingly . In Israel, who commissions a poll has predicted which way it leans , and pollsters partly follow one another , so firms with shared ownership or methodology are modeled as correlated voices, not independent ones.

Methodology

The statistical machinery, end to end

The pipeline below is a genuine sequence — each stage consumes the last. Parameters measured on the record come from eight election cycles (2009–2022) and are graded leave-one-cycle-out ; judgment parameters are stated and cited, and the limitations chapter lists the ones the record cannot test. Every claim cites its evidence; the citations open the Sources chapter.

The system at a glance

The forecast is one pipeline: four data assets feed a Bayesian poll model of the kind used in multiparty forecasting ; its posterior is pushed through a stack of election-day shocks measured on Israel's own record ; the resulting vote-share draws are made physically lawful, mixed over the registration events still open, passed through the real seat-allocation law , and finally through an explicit government-formation tree . Every parameter is either measured on the 2009–2022 record (graded leave-one-cycle-out) or declared as a documented judgment in the decision log. Nothing in the chain is hand-tuned to make the answer look reasonable. The chapter The evidence behind each layer sorts every layer by the kind of evidence behind it; the chapter after it lists what the evidence cannot yet settle.

The poll database

1,918 polls, 23,661 list-level observations, nine cycles (2009–2026), scraped from the Wikipedia polling archives with a purpose-built parser (grid/colspan handling, two-digit years, US-style dates, result-row detection), then reconciled against a canonical party registry that tracks every list's mergers, splits, and bloc identity across 17 years — so "Yamina 2021" and "New Right 2019" resolve to comparable objects. The archive's same-day "a/b" variants of one survey (with and without a list, joint versus split Arab lists) are collapsed to the most complete configuration, so one sample is never counted twice. For the 2026 cycle, the raw poll reports that pollsters must legally file with the Central Elections Committee are scraped directly from gov.il: 103 filings parsed, and where a filing matches a published poll its raw vote shares displace the seat-derived numbers (72 polls, 1,123 observations, currently displaced) — the reference aggregators fit raw shares, not processed seat tables . The small parties that pollsters report only in footnotes are itemized and carried explicitly — see the small-parties chapter . A second observation stream carries sector-specialist polls (Afkar, StatNet): a share of the Arab sector converts to a national share through the sector's measured weight in the vote and the fraction of each list's vote that comes from that sector (kalpi poststrat), and enters the fit as a poll by the sector firm with extra noise for the conversion (decision log §20) — precisely the voters mainstream panels under-reach: most national polls are online panels of 500–600 whose Arab subsamples are small and self-selected, and the national election study itself tops up its Arab sample by phone . The Arab-sector pollster has no published turnout or seat track record, so the extra noise is a judgment .

vℓ = sharesector · Wsector / concℓ    sector poll → national share (W = eligible × turnout / total votes)

House effects and firm reliability

Each firm's bloc lean is estimated leave-own-pollster-out: firm f's polls are compared with the contemporaneous average of all other firms, then shrunk toward zero in proportion to sample size (empirical Bayes). Level and house effects cannot both be learned from polls alone, so the leans are centred across firms and the industry's shared error is carried by the election-day shocks instead . The chart in the polling-industry section above plots the result. Israeli media-sponsored polls in 2019–20 leaned in the direction of the sponsoring outlet's stance on Netanyahu , and pollsters partly take cues from one another . Firms with common ownership or lineage — the two halves of the former Filber/Direct Polls operation, Lazar/Panels Politics — therefore share a correlation group: their errors are modeled as one voice, so five agreeing polls from one polling family never masquerade as five independent confirmations.

δ̂f = nf/(nf+k) · meant( yf,t − ȳ−f,t )    house effect, EB-shrunk
λf ~ LogNormal(accuracyf, σƒ),  σp = sobs·λf·eβuup    precision weight from the final-poll record

Accuracy scorecards from eight graded cycles set each firm's precision prior λ — and the scorecard grades each firm's final pre-election poll against the result (election-day exit polls are excluded, and a firm graded on few cycles is shrunk toward the industry median, because past accuracy predicts future accuracy only modestly ), so the weight rewards being right when it counts. Weighting by the graded record rather than by sample size follows the evidence that opt-in panels' main defect is bias, not variance, and that a textbook margin of error does not apply to them . A firm with a loud record gets a wide noise term; its polls still move the model, just less. One caution the audit surfaced: each panel's polls scatter only about half a seat around the firm's own trend on the bloc total, against 2–3 seats of pure sampling error . Part of that smoothness may be design efficiency rather than stickiness — weighting on past vote can push a poll's design effect below one — so the precision prior is held tight enough that within-cycle smoothness cannot overturn a firm's graded record. A hard sampling-error floor on every poll was built, tested on the eight-cycle harness, and rejected: it widened already-wide intervals and cost accuracy , the verdict Linzer reached on inflating poll noise for US state polls . House effects are not frozen either: each firm's lean carries a drift term — a per-firm slope over the campaign window, centred across firms so a common drift stays in the latent trend — because a firm's methodology and sample can move between April and October; a bias that moves linearly in time is the form used for US state polls (decision log §21).

δf,ℓ(t) = δf,ℓ + sf,ℓ · (t − t̄)/T,  Σf sf,ℓ = 0    time-varying house effect

The Bayesian state-space model

The production model is a latent-trajectory model in the state-space tradition — a hidden trend, read through polls that each carry their firm's estimated lean — fitted with the No-U-Turn sampler (PyMC). State-space aggregation has been tested in multiparty systems such as Germany and Sweden . Each list's true support follows a random walk in centred-log-ratio space : logs taken against the geometric mean of all lists, so the shares stay between 0 and 1, sum to 100%, and every list is treated symmetrically — no list's movement depends on which list happens to be the reference . The walk runs on a non-uniform time grid — weekly bins, tightening to 2-day bins in the final month. The current 2026 fit consumes 173 polls, 2,188 observations, 15 modeled lists, 45 time bins, 10 firms in 6 correlation groups.

zt = zt−1 + √Δt · τ · εt,  Σℓ zt,ℓ = 0    latent support, centred log-ratio (CLR) space
yp,ℓ ~ StudentT(4, softmax(zt(p))ℓ + βg(p) + δf(p), σp)    poll observation
σp = sobs · λf(p) · exp(βu · up)    noise scales with firm record + undecideds

House effects β nest within pollster correlation groups; firm weights λ carry LogNormal priors centered on each firm's historical accuracy; βu is an estimated coefficient letting reported undecided shares widen a poll's noise. Each poll is allowed error beyond its sampling margin, estimated from the data, as in the Economist's US model : in two-party and multiparty systems alike, real poll error runs about twice the stated margin . The likelihood is fat-tailed (Student-t), so one aberrant number cannot drag the trend . The current fit's realised diagnostics: 0 divergences, r̂ ≤ 1.02, bulk ESS ≥ 332 — divergences are the sampler's alarm for regions it cannot explore . A refit ships only if it passes gates of r̂ ≤ 1.02 and bulk ESS ≥ 300, looser than the published standard of r̂ below 1.01 with bulk and tail ESS above 400 ; tightening them is on the open list. A live holdout — refit without the last 14 days — predicts the held-out polls with 0.73 seats median error.

The campaign volatility calendar

Forward uncertainty is not a constant: it follows the calendar. Across 45 countries, polls become steadily more informative about the vote as election day approaches , and in systems as fragmented as Israel's a real share of the movement still comes in the final 50 days . Measured on Israel's own record across seven cycles (decision log §12), the list-formation window (77–36 days out) moves 1.3–1.5 seats per list per week; the final five weeks move only 0.6–0.75. The projection's forward variance is scaled accordingly — intervals tighten automatically once lists close on September 8, not because we assume calm but because the historical record says the race calms . Late movement is treated as real, not as noise to revert: in six of eight past elections the final weeks' poll movement brought the average closer to the result .

Poll herding was measured and deliberately not double-counted: final-week dispersion runs ~0.88× mid-campaign (decision log §12), but the election-day shocks below are fitted on final-poll errors that already contain it. Pollsters do take cues from one another , so inside the fit the polls still count as more independent than they are — listed under limitations. No new poll may be published from the end of Friday 23 October until the polls close ; those last four days are carried by the election-day shocks, which are fitted on past final-poll errors that contain the same blind window.

The error decomposition

Polls miss elections in structured ways. Real poll error is about twice the quoted margin of error, and much of it is shared by every poll in the race, so averaging more polls does not remove it ; German and British multiparty forecasts proved overconfident when their election-day error term was missing or too small . Israel is not in the main cross-national poll-error dataset, so every shock size below comes from Israel's own eight graded elections . A sequential decomposition of those cycles (each estimated leave-one-cycle-out, so no cycle grades itself) splits final-poll error into orthogonal layers that become the projection's shocks — nothing is double-counted:

v = softmax( zE + swing·b + shocksector + anchor + resid + gevalt + undecided )    projected vote share
  • Bloc swing — the correlated right/left error, with mean zero: Israeli polls have not systematically under-counted the right as a bloc , and poll errors line up within an election but not in the same direction from one election to the next . Its scale is the record's bloc-error spread (3.36 seats), drawn from a t-distribution with 5 degrees of freedom because big misses on the gap between the camps are not rare and fat tails are standard practice . This cycle it is multiplied by 1.2 for event risk, a judgment: security shocks do move Israeli votes, in either direction , but the allowance cannot be tested on past elections, and where it sits in the model is an open question (see Limitations);
  • Sector shocks — haredi and Arab voters are the hardest for Israeli pollsters to reach , and an audit of the 2006–2013 polls found both sectors' parties under-estimated . The haredi lists share a shock measured on the record: their final polls under-counted them by 0.98 seats on the raw record and 1.35 net of the bloc swing ; the projection draws a mean under-count of 1.35 seats with sd 1.77 (decision log §51). The mean rests on two large misses, and almost all of it is Shas's . The Arab lists carry a graded turnout term instead of a fixed seat shift, because Arab-list poll error is mainly a turnout problem and Arab turnout has tracked whether the Arab parties run together . Each simulation draws an Arab-sector turnout around 58%: the leave-one-out mean of 2015, September 2019 and 2020 (a unified four-party Joint List: 63.5, 59.2 and 64.8%) and 2021 (the Joint List without Ra'am: 44.6%) , a value the sector's own 2025–26 surveys bracket . Only part of the expected change against the turnout the polls are assumed to embed (53.8%, the 2022 regular-station figure) reaches the vote — the fitted pass-through is 0.25 — because the record shows final polls already absorb most of a turnout swing, plus a general under-count of 0.63 seats ; the draw's own spread passes through in full, and the Joint List and Ra'am move together, taking the mass from every other list. Graded strictly leave-one-cycle-out, this form beat both the earlier full pass-through and the old additive shift on the pooled scores; it ships on the harness's pooled route, not on every fold . The haredi and Arab tides share a correlated draw (the two sectors' turnout swings correlate at about 0.9 over four transitions, carried by both sectors' 2021 dip ; shrunk to 0.5);
  • Anchors — measured, then retired. Likud beat its final polls by about 2.3 seats on average, but almost all of that came from 2015 and April 2019; in the other six cycles its miss ran from about −3 to +2 seats . Applied as fixed shifts, the anchors cost accuracy on the eight-cycle harness, because a mean of two big years and six small ones is wrong six times in eight . They are off. What replaced them is conditional: Likud's big beats came when it was trailing the largest non-bloc list in the final polls — behind the Zionist Union in 2015, behind Blue and White in April 2019, tied in September 2019 — and in those years the bloc totals stayed close to their final polls while Likud gained at its own satellites' expense . A threatened loss mobilises strong partisans , and voters who see the race for first place as close switch toward a would-be formateur . When Likud's projected margin over the largest non-bloc list is 0.5 seats or less, the simulation adds a share of that trailing-year beat (mean +2.5 seats, wide), taken from Likud's own non-haredi satellites (Otzma, RZP–Zehut and Noam; at most 75% of their vote). Sourced that way, the layer improved every pooled score on the eight-cycle harness when it was graded and stopped adding seats to the Netanyahu bloc as a whole ; today's margin in the published run is 0.43 seats;
  • New lists at the line — in Israeli election studies small parties lose votes to the major ones , and avoiding a wasted vote is one of the strategic contexts a threshold creates . Measured on the horizon record (every cycle projected as of D-56 and D-21): of eleven first-cycle lists projected within the threshold band, two passed and nine failed , a mean drift of −1.6 points of seat-implied share. Seat-implied share scores a list that failed at zero; scored on the vote shares the failed lists actually won, the drift is far smaller, so the term is under re-measurement (established lists at the line are calibrated). A first-cycle list projected within −0.75/+1.5 points of 3.25% therefore carries that desertion (mean −1.6, sd 2.6), which improved the straddler Brier score on the horizon record with established lists unchanged . Near-threshold lists can surge as well as desert , and supporters discount bad polls for their own small list — why a one-signed term is on the open list. Today it applies to Amcha and the Reservists–Economic list;
  • Residual per-list shock — how hard a party is to poll depends on the party itself, its size and whether it is new , and big parties carry the biggest misses in points . The residual is 1.83 + 0.044·seats, scaled by a width factor of 0.9 and multiplied by 1.35 for first-cycle lists (measured: new parties' final polls are unbiased but noisier) . A loyal-electorate calibration shrinks lists with a long, stable record to their measured total volatility: Shas 1.5, United Torah Judaism 1.3, Yisrael Beytenu 2.2, Likud 4.8 seats (decision log §7; Yisrael Beytenu's final-poll record shows no Russian-speaker bias ). The fit of this layer is under revision: threshold deaths and an indexing error in the decomposition inflate its intercept, and Israeli list errors are fat-tailed while the draw is Gaussian ;
  • Certainty layer — a sizeable share of voters settle their choice only in the final two months , and certainty is lowest in the political centre . Each list's soft support (its supporters who are not certain: Together 47% locked and Amcha 38% are measured, the rest declared tiers) churns at a defection rate drawn per simulation (mean 0.25) with list-specific spread, so soft lists carry asymmetric downside — a new list's soft intention can fade heavily, as Kadima's did in 2006 . Where churn lands is measured from the 2021→2022 voter-transition matrix (Kolot Nodedim, 7,035 matched stations): of change-bloc voters who left their list, 72.5% chose another change list, 10.3% crossed, 17.2% abstained; for the Netanyahu bloc 85.5 / 6.6 / 7.9. The undecided pool is measured from the CEC filings of the firms that report a genuine undecided share (median of the last 45 days, ~10%). Undecided voters are not assumed to split like decided ones : their lean is pooled over every published undecided-lean measurement with a proportional-split prior of equal n — proportional allocation is a defensible default — today N12's 55/16 (~43 leaned respondents), so ~0.7 seats per side move from the Netanyahu bloc to the change bloc in expectation (~1.4 seats of gap), and every new wave shifts the posterior by its own n. This is the least-tested layer: the profession regards the allocation of undecideds as unsettled , no Israeli house has a track record of lean measurements predicting final-poll error , the one measured Israeli late-decider break (2015) went right , most of today's undecideds are choosing within a bloc , and so far the polls have moved without the drift the lean implies ;
  • Gevalt coupling — a zero-sum transfer (sd 2.0 seats) between Likud and its own bloc's satellites, so satellite threshold deaths co-occur with Likud surges, as in 2015 and 2019: every Israeli shortfall of four seats or more since 2009 was a smaller list in the surging leader's own bloc , and the late 2015 surge was a transfer inside the right-wing bloc . Graded on the harness it was neutral on accuracy and is kept as structure .

The kalpi substrate

Beneath the national numbers sits the polling-station record: official CEC results for every locality in the five elections 2019–2022, matched to party lineages through each cycle's ballot letters (verified against national totals, so a wrong letter cannot silently poison the panel). Localities cluster into political sectors by their own vote profiles, along with their measured turnout (haredi 76%, Arab 54%, religious-Zionist 76%); the haredi cluster's turnout is high and nearly flat across 2019–2022 . Three products feed the forecast: a poststratification table allocating each list's seats to the sectors it draws from (the Ground Game section); the capacity truncation below; and the locality baseline for the election-night engine. The lineage weights are calibrated against the measured voter-transition matrices of the open-source Kolot Nodedim project (~7,000 matched stations per election pair, R² 0.89 for 2021→2022), whose per-party cohesion estimates independently reproduce this model's loyal-electorate ordering (decision log §10).

vShas + vUTJ ≥ 6.6%    haredi floor

Simulations that violate either bound are clipped to the feasible boundary. Both bounds are looser than their form suggests: the Arab ceiling implicitly assumes every Arab-town voter backs an Arab list, and Shas and UTJ draw under a third of their vote from the haredi cluster, so the haredi floor is a judgment rather than a physical limit; the ceiling binds in a few percent of draws and the floor almost never . One honest negative result is recorded alongside: sector-level swings do not transfer across election pairs (uniform national swing predicts localities at 1.20pp mean error; adding historical sector swings gives 1.31pp; decision log §9) — so the sector layer informs correlations and capacity, not point swings.

The small parties

Every list on the ballot that the pollsters measure is modelled the same way, however small . The Haredi Public, Noam, Israel First, Ahi, Tzomet Beit Yisrael and Code Black are full members of the poll model, exactly like Likud: each has its own latent trend, its own house effects and its own election-day shock, fed by the pollsters' itemized footnotes and by the raw legal filings, which carry every list's percentage. A list needs at least three polls or filings in the window to be identified; lists that did not file for the ballot are dropped, and Ale Yarok's readings after its 3 September union are added to Israel First. This is how the eight-cycle harness has always treated the record's small lists that had their own poll column (Yachad 2015, Otzma September 2019, Jewish Home 2022), so the 2026 small lists are now graded by the same machinery that grades the large ones. Strategic desertion, voters fleeing a wasted vote , comes from the layers that act on every list: the certainty layer moves soft support, and first-cycle lists inside the threshold band carry the measured straddler term. The record shows why both matter: the New Economic Party polled about 1.2% in 2021 and took 0.79% , and Jewish Home polled 1.2–2.1% in the final polls of 2022 and took 1.19% . Arab and haredi sectoral lists held or beat their final polls — Balad polled 1.0–2.75% in 2022 and took 2.91% — but right-flank lists did not when the bloc leader campaigned against wasted votes: Otzma Yehudit polled at or near the threshold in September 2019 and took 1.88% , and Zehut polled 4–8 seats in April 2019 and took 2.74% . Zehut is no longer carried separately at all: since September 2 the pollsters publish a single Religious Zionism–Zehut column, so its support lives directly inside that line's poll stream.

Threshold and Bader–Ofer

Each simulated vote vector passes the 3.25% threshold test — 3.25% of all valid votes, tested list by list . The threshold has been 3.25% since the 2015 election and was 2% in 2006–2013 ; the backtest applies each cycle's own threshold. Seats are then allocated by the actual Israeli method — Bader–Ofer, which is d'Hondt over the lists that pass — with signed surplus-vote agreements treated as joint lists at the remainder stage, and only when both partners pass on their own . Pairs are a real but one-seat-sized mechanism: only 3 to 8 of the 120 seats per election are decided at the remainder stage , and on the 2015–2022 record a pair is worth about +0.4 expected seats, gaining a seat in roughly a third of outcomes . The implementation reproduces the official allocation of every election since 2015 exactly ; an exact tie, never observed, would go to a lottery run by the Central Elections Committee . Threshold failures redistribute their votes to survivors, which is how a bloc can lose the vote and win the Knesset: in 2022 almost 9% of votes were wasted, and a Netanyahu bloc some 30,000 votes ahead won 64 seats . Israel's gap between votes and seats comes from votes wasted below the threshold, not from the d'Hondt formula . The published central seats are this law applied once to the mixture's median vote-share vector, so the table sums to 120 with no rounding or floors.

seatk → argmaxℓ vℓ / (sℓ + 1)    d’Hondt iteration, surplus pairs pooled

The formation tree

Seats are not power. Coalition outcomes depend on who the actors are, what they want and the constraints on their bargaining, not on seat arithmetic alone , and in the comparative record pre-election pacts and public refusals to govern together are among the strongest predictors of which cabinet forms . Every simulated Knesset therefore runs through an explicit formation sequence — an ordered ladder of gates, each with behavioral parameters derived from institutional research: who actually signs for each party (Shas's Council, UTJ's two rebbes' councils, Ra'am's post-Shura independence), each leader's revealed record at past forks (Liberman's flips , Bennett's 2021 pledge-break, Gantz's 2020), and historical base rates. Since 2019 Shas and UTJ have tied themselves to Netanyahu's bloc in writing, which is why their defection priors are low . Winter's Amcha is coded as an anti-Eisenkot hard edge: it can join a Bennett-led centre government but essentially never an Eisenkot-led one. On the right Winter recommends Netanyahu but joins only if a national service law passes first (INN, 2 Sept), so the tree counts Amcha's seats in his coalition in about 60% of simulations, while the bloc totals at the top of the page count them in full. Hendel's Reservists–Economic list counts only toward a coalition with no haredi or Arab partner, his stated rule (JPost); in 2020 it was Hendel who helped block a Joint List-backed minority government . The change lists campaign for a "Zionist government" that does not rely on Arab parties at the outset . The behavioral parameters themselves carry uncertainty — each simulation draws its own set from Beta/Dirichlet priors, the scenario-tree counterpart of the random coefficients that fit formation data better than fixed ones — so the formation probabilities come with honest intervals, shown in the scenario board; even the best statistical models name the exact cabinet only about one time in three . And the gates are configuration-conditional: the declared probabilities are marginal means, and each simulated parliament's gate probability moves with its seat map through a logistic — haredi defection rises with the right's gap to 61 and when that party alone closes the centre's gap (Shas joined non-Likud prime ministers in 1992, 1999 and 2006, and refused in 2021 ), a centrist defection falls as the gap grows (Gantz 2020 at a gap of three), unity rises with deadlock depth. The conditional gates cannot yet be graded: the formation harness runs an older parameter set, and three of its eight cycles are certain by construction, so only five outcomes inform it . They are retained on institutional grounds, with the marginals preserved by construction.

The PM allocation

Within each winning coalition, the premiership is allocated with rotation-aware, scenario-specific probabilities, flipping when Together outpolls Yashar in a draw. The largest list usually supplies the prime minister — in 7 of the 10 Israeli governments formed since 1996, about the comparative rate; the exceptions were 1996 (direct election), 2009 and 2021, when Bennett became prime minister with 7 seats . Of Israel's three rotation deals, one never reached its handover date, so the model's prime minister is the first one sworn in . This layer has no current grade: the formation harness that scored it used a superseded parameter set , and a regrade is part of the formation calibration under way.

Expert priors, documented

Every judgment number traces to the decision log, compiled from six research desks: coalition-formation base rates 1992–2022, the complete Israeli polling-error record 1996–2022, party institutions and leader biographies, electoral sociology and vote flows, prediction markets and fundamentals, and final-stretch campaign dynamics. Where the desks disagreed with the model, the model moved. A second review then checked every method and empirical claim on this site against the literature and official data, in eight further desks with an independent verification pass: the Sources chapter lists what survived, and the limitations chapter lists where that literature challenges a choice the model has not changed. Exchange markets are compared, not fitted . Polymarket priced Eisenkot-as-PM at roughly 50–60% in late August, against the model's 21%; but its contract resolves to whoever is sworn in after the 2026 election, possibly after a repeat election, so it does not price the same event . Kalshi's same-definition contracts leave little room for a repeat election, far below the model's figure; that gap is listed under limitations .

Validation: the model grades itself

Every layer is gated on an eight-cycle backtest (2009–2022): each election is predicted with parameters estimated from the other seven (leave-one-group-out cross-validation ), and folds are whole elections, never individual polls, because polls within a campaign are not independent . The harness re-runs the full pipeline as of each election eve and scores it against the real result: seat error per list, coverage of the 90% ranges, the interval score, which charges for a range's width and twenty-fold for every seat by which the result falls outside it , and the Brier score for clearing the threshold . Changes that failed the harness were rejected and documented — including a final-stretch dynamics module, a hierarchical multi-cycle fit that stays research-only, and a per-poll sampling-error floor . A second, horizon-aware harness refits every cycle as of 56, 21 and 7 days out ; the table below gives the current numbers. Its clearest finding is that the 90% ranges over-cover at every horizon. That is not a safety margin: a calibrated forecast covers about 90%, and over-coverage means the ranges are wider than they need to be (part of the excess is mechanical, because seat counts are whole numbers ). The harness calibrates the width of the per-list residual by re-projecting every fitted cycle under a grid of width factors; the latest grid chose a narrower width than the shipped one in every leave-one-cycle-out fold , so retuning the width is part of the current revision. The harness projection mirrors the production shock stack layer for layer (bloc swing, correlated sector shocks including the Arab turnout term, the trailing-Likud and new-straddler terms, residual, loyal-electorate calibration, gevalt coupling); the volatility calendar is graded by the horizon harness only; the certainty layer, event risk and the capacity bounds rest on 2026 measurements or judgment and are outside its reach by construction, and the annex says so where they appear. These backtest numbers describe a configuration that was selected on the same eight elections, so they are a record of historical performance, not a clean out-of-sample estimate .

Limitations and open questions

What the model does not yet do, and what the evidence cannot settle. The model is mid-revision: a fourth accuracy round is testing the interval width, a fix to the residual fit, seat-law fixes (the threshold denominator and the backtest's registered surplus pairs), the calibration of the formation tree, the sector terms and event risk. Every model setting quoted in this annex is filled from the current build, and the decision log records each change and its grade.

Challenges the model has not adopted

The research review found these challenges from the literature to hold, wholly or in part. None has been adopted yet; several are under test in the current round.

  • Poll model. The centred-log-ratio walk has one direction the data cannot see, which can slow the sampler; a sum-to-zero parameterisation removes it . The lists' latent movements are independent, where the reference multiparty model lets them covary and so learns within-bloc substitution instead of declaring it . Robustness acts per list cell, so a poll that is wrong as a whole is down-weighted only in its extreme cells . House effects are re-learned each cycle, though in Spain they persist across elections . Only each firm's final poll per cycle is graded, while a record is mostly noise until about thirty graded polls . Herding is measured but not priced, so the fit treats polls as more independent than they are .
  • Election-day shocks. Event risk multiplies an election-day error that is as large a week out as a month out and never decays with the calendar; the evidence places event risk in the forward walk, fading to nothing at the poll blackout . The fat-tailed draws on the bloc and family shocks widen them beyond their measured spread . The per-list residual is Gaussian while Israeli list errors are fat-tailed, and its intercept is inflated by threshold deaths and by an indexing error in the fit ; new lists near the threshold receive both the wider new-list residual and the desertion term . Layers fitted on three to eleven cases (trailing-Likud, new straddlers, the Arab term) enter at their point estimates instead of being drawn from their own uncertainty .
  • Sectors. The haredi mean is fragile and almost all Shas's . The Arab under-count is Ra'am-shaped, while the term moves both Arab lists together . Two pre-registered candidates were graded on these points: the raw haredi mean was neutral on the backtest, and moving the Arab intercept onto Ra'am improved Ra'am but failed the shipping rule, so neither shipped (decision log §51). Beyond that, the Arab turnout mean leans on years of a unified Joint List, and the turnout the polls themselves embed is an untested assumption ; the Arab capacity ceiling is loose .
  • Strategic behaviour. The new-straddler term is one-signed, but the record and the threshold-insurance literature show surges too . The trailing-Likud trigger is trailing, while the panel evidence points to the closeness of the race for first place, which can favour either bloc's leader . The gevalt coupling is symmetric and always on, while the record's transfers run one way, in close races . The undecided lean rests on one July measurement with no Israeli track record .
  • Seat law. The threshold test renormalises over the modelled lists, so its denominator leaves out the micro-list vote . The backtest's historical surplus pairs approximate the registered agreements, so part of its seat error is seat-law error rather than poll error .
  • Formation. The repeat-election outcome sits well above the historical rate of failed formations and far above same-definition exchange prices . Shas and UTJ count as certain members of a Netanyahu coalition, although UTJ quit his government and Shas's ministers resigned over the draft law in July 2025 . Ra'am's entry and a unity government without Netanyahu are priced from precedents that came only after repeated elections . The formation harness cannot grade the shipped tree .
  • Evaluation. The shipping rule mixes a per-fold route and a pooled route, without a paired standard error, and several layers were chosen among variants on the folds that then grade them . A gate that forbids losing coverage rewards over-wide ranges . The backtest scores one 90% range and pooled Brier scores, not whole distributions or the joint seat outcome that drives the bloc probability . The graded point estimate is not the printed central count . Model checking is one in-sample predictive check and one fake-data run on an earlier model .

The eight-cycle record is thin

Eight elections, 2009–2022, are the whole graded Israeli record . With about ten independent events, any success rate between 0.5 and 0.9 is consistent with a 0.7 forecast; lists within an election are correlated; and tails cannot be checked from past data at all . Four of the eight folds (April 2019 to 2021) fell within 23 months with largely the same lists, so they are not independent of one another . Leave-one-cycle-out also lets later elections inform earlier ones: the trailing-Likud mean, the new-straddler drift, the Arab term and the industry shocks all borrow from later cycles, and the firm scorecards are built from the whole record; a leave-future-out grade is not yet published . Because the backtest also selected the configuration it then scores, its numbers describe historical performance rather than estimate future accuracy . The 2026 election is the one truly unseen test .

Monte Carlo precision of the headline

Every probability on the page is a share of 8,000 simulated Knessets. Sampling alone gives the Netanyahu-majority figure a Monte Carlo standard error of about 0.4 points, and different projection seeds move it by more than that ; the sampler's own convergence gates are also looser than the published standard . Read headline probabilities to the nearest whole point, not to the decimal shown. A win probability also reads as more certain than the vote shares behind it , which is why each probability on the page sits next to the seat range it comes from.

Israeli evidence gaps

Israel is absent from the main cross-national poll-error dataset and from the out-of-sample coalition-formation models . The one peer-reviewed study of Israeli poll bias in the 2019–2022 era is known here only from its abstract, without effect sizes . Arab-sector turnout surveys have missed by 4 to 15 points in both directions, and stated intention to vote overstates turnout ; the Arab-sector pollster has no published track record . No Israeli house has a track record for undecided-lean measurements . There is no quantitative estimate of the turnout machinery lost when the High Court barred passing who-has-voted data to the parties in September . The 2026 double-envelope count adds evacuee stations that no 2019–2022 base rate contains . Several Israeli findings on strategic voting are known only through secondary accounts or abstracts .
